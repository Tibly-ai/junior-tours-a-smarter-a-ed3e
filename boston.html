Boston

Boston feels like a living textbook. Cobblestone streets, landmark meeting halls, and stories that shaped the nation wait around every corner. Alongside its Revolutionary roots, the city is known for world-class universities, inspiring museums, and neighborhoods packed with character.

From Boston Common and Faneuil Hall to Fenway Park, students get a trip that blends history, culture, and plenty of energy.

Highlights

Boston Common

The country's oldest public park, 45 acres that have served as a cow pasture, a military camp, and the site of hangings, protest marches, and visits by dignitaries. Today the Common marks the first stop on the Freedom Trail.

Bunker Hill Monument

Standing 221 feet tall, this monument marks the site of the first major battle of the American Revolution.

Near Boston

Battlefields of Lexington & Concord

Visit the Minuteman Statue, the Old Burying Ground, and the National Historical Parks as students learn about the first battle of the Revolutionary War.

Walden Pond

Henry David Thoreau lived at Walden Pond from 1845 to 1847. His time there produced the book Walden, which helped inspire a lasting respect for the natural world.
