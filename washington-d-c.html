Washington D.C.

Washington, D.C. puts your students face-to-face with our nation's past, present, and future. Established in 1790 as the capital of a new country, the District today offers unmatched access to museums, monuments, and memorials that make classroom learning feel real.

From the Declaration of Independence to time-honored traditions like the Changing of the Guard, D.C. gives students a chance to stand where history actually happened.

Washington D.C. Landmarks

US Capitol

Construction began in 1793, and for over a century it was the only building built for the nation's legislature.

Lincoln Memorial

Standing at the west end of the National Mall, this neoclassical monument honors the 16th President and features his Gettysburg Address.

Washington Monument

Built between 1848 and 1884, the obelisk honors the nation's first president and remains one of D.C.'s most recognizable landmarks.

Vietnam Veterans Memorial

This memorial honors members of the U.S. armed forces who died in service or remain unaccounted for from the Vietnam War.

World War II Memorial

Symbolic of the defining event of the 20th century, this memorial recognizes the spirit, sacrifice, and commitment of the American people.

Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial

This memorial honors a man of courage who devoted his life to justice, democracy, and hope.

Arlington National Cemetery

Students can watch the Changing of the Guard at the Tomb of the Unknowns and visit the grave sites of President John F. Kennedy, Senator Robert F. Kennedy, and the crew of the Challenger.

Library of Congress

Started by Congress in 1800 with 740 books and 3 maps sent from London, the collection was later burned by the British before being rebuilt into the world's largest library.

More to Explore

D.C. offers dozens of additional stops that make every itinerary feel fresh, from the Smithsonian museums to the National Archives.

Monuments & Memorials

FDR Memorial

Dedicated on May 2, 1997, this memorial spans 7.5 acres, tracing 12 years of history through four outdoor rooms, one for each of FDR's terms in office.

Jefferson Memorial

Modeled after the Pantheon in Rome, the Thomas Jefferson Memorial stands as America's foremost tribute to our third president.

Korean War Memorial

This memorial features 19 larger-than-life statues representing a patrol of soldiers, honoring those who served in the Korean War.
